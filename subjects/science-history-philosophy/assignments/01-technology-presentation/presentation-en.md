# Technology and the Philosophy of Technology

*From tools and crafts to technological determinism* (Based on the lecture by Haidar Alphy)

## 2. What is technology?

- **Etymology** – Greek techne – art, or craft-knowledge
- **Knowledge** – The body of knowledge associated with a particular practice
- **Tools** – The creation of tools and objects
- **Impact** – Extends our natural abilities and alters our social environment

## 3. Technology in everyday life

- **Navigation** – GPS finds the fastest route to a destination
- **Recognition** – Facebook can recognize your face
- **Remote services** – Cab, flight, payment, music, film or game – all done remotely

Technology raises the efficiency and pleasure of our personal lives.

## 4. Philosophy of technology

- **Early thinkers** – Plato, Aristotle, Francis Bacon, Martin Heidegger
- **2nd half of the 19th century** – A field studying the most general laws of technology as a broad phenomenon
- **1990s** – The ‘empirical turn’: actual technologies in relation to science, people and society

## 5. Technological reality: a double-edged sword

- **Invention** – discovery
- **Innovation** – first commercial application
- **Diffusion** – widespread replication and growth

- Endless progress vs. limited natural resources
- Technological systems are interconnected webs
- Speed, power, complexity

## 6. Ethical issues

- Technologies challenge philosophical concepts and theories
- Dangerous, unpredictable, even disastrous – ‘demonic technology’
- Is ethics only for humans, or is there room for technologies too?

- **Example** – Deep brain implants interfere with mood and behaviour, challenging our ideas of autonomy, free will and what it means to ‘act’.

## 7. Technological determinism

**Social change is initiated by technology**

- Advances mark each new phase of human history – the wheel, the computer
- Once invented, technologies take on ‘a life of their own’
- Society is forced to adapt

## 8. Future shock

> The dizzying disorientation brought on by the premature arrival of the future. – Alvin Toffler

Change is so fast that society’s fundamental values become blurred.

## 9. Four types of new technologies

- **Connectivity and computing** – Cloud, Internet, blockchain, sensors
- **Analytics and intelligence** – Advanced analytics, machine learning, AI
- **Human–machine interaction** – VR and AR, robotics, automation, autonomous vehicles
- **Advanced engineering** – 3-D printing, renewable energy, nanoparticles

Work changes too: upskilling – learning new knowledge; reskilling – retraining for different positions.

## 10. Conclusions

> Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic. – Arthur C. Clarke

- Technology extends human abilities and reshapes society
- It is a double-edged sword and raises real ethical questions
- Constant learning keeps us ready for the future
